Significant Figures

A balance reading communicates more than a number: the digits shown also tell us how finely the measurement was reported. Significant figures help us report results without implying unsupported precision.

ObjectivesConceptsPracticeKey takeaways

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Count significant figures in measurements, including values with zeros.
  • Distinguish measured quantities from exact counts and defined conversion factors.
  • Round values to a specified number of significant figures.
  • Apply appropriate reporting rules to addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and mixed calculations.

1. What do significant figures communicate?

In a reported measurement, significant figures are the digits used to express its measured value, including the last reported digit, which has uncertainty. They indicate the measurement’s reported precision; they do not by themselves establish accuracy or a complete uncertainty range.

For example, 8.2 g and 8.20 g express the same numerical value, but 8.20 g is reported to the hundredths place. Do not add a zero to a measured value unless the measurement supports it.

Make the intended precision clear

Write 2.4 × 10³ for two significant figures, 2.40 × 10³ for three, or 2.400 × 10³ for four. In normalized scientific notation, all digits written in the coefficient are significant.

Exact values: Counts such as 12 objects and defined conversion factors such as 1 m = 100 cm are exact. They do not limit the significant figures in a calculation using measured values.

3. Rounding a result

  1. Identify the last digit to keep.
  2. Look at the first digit to be discarded. In this course, use the common rule: 5 or greater rounds the retained digit up; less than 5 leaves it unchanged.
  3. Keep place value clear, using scientific notation when needed.

Examples

6.783 to three significant figures is 6.78. The next digit is 3.

0.009476 to two significant figures is 0.0095. The leading zeros do not count; 4 rounds up because the next digit is 7.

9.96 to two significant figures is 10., or more clearly 1.0 × 10¹.

In a multistep calculation, retain extra digits in intermediate results and round at the end, while tracking the limiting precision at each step.

4. Reporting calculated answers

Multiplication and division

Round the final result to the same number of significant figures as the measured input with the fewest significant figures.

Example 1 · Density

Mass = 12.45 g; volume = 3.2 cm³.

ρ = 12.45 ÷ 3.2 = 3.890625 g/cm³ → 3.9 g/cm³.

The volume has two significant figures, so the final answer has two.

Addition and subtraction

Round the result to the least precise place value among the measured inputs. For values written with decimals, this is the fewest decimal places.

Example 2 · Mass by difference

Container with sample = 37.68 g; empty container = 8.4 g.

Sample mass = 37.68 − 8.4 = 29.28 g → 29.3 g, to the tenths place.

Example 3 · A mixed calculation

A container and sample have a mass of 25.46 g; the empty container has a mass of 10.2 g. The sample occupies 5.00 mL.

Unrounded mass difference = 15.26 g, limited to the tenths place (15.3 g). Keeping guard digits: 15.26 ÷ 5.00 = 3.052 g/mL → 3.05 g/mL. The mass difference is effectively reliable to three significant figures and the volume has three.

For values in scientific notation, match exponents before applying the addition/subtraction place-value rule. Significant-figure rules are useful conventions; formal uncertainty analysis can provide a more complete treatment.

Key terms and takeaways

  • Significant figures communicate the precision of a reported measurement.
  • Leading zeros are not significant; zeros between nonzero digits and trailing zeros after a decimal are significant.
  • Exact counts and defined conversions do not limit significant figures.
  • Multiplication and division use the fewest significant figures; addition and subtraction use the least precise place value.
  • Keep guard digits during intermediate steps and round the final result.
