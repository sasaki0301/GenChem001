Scientific Measurement: Mass and Volume

How can a small object have more mass than a larger one? Begin by measuring two different properties: how much matter an object contains and how much space it occupies.

ObjectivesMeasurementMassVolumePracticeKey takeaways

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Distinguish mass from volume and describe how each is measured.
  • Select suitable instruments and units for common measurements.
  • Calculate the volume of a rectangular solid and find the volume of an irregular solid by liquid displacement.
  • Record measurements and results with appropriate units.
Coming next: In Lesson 2, we will combine mass and volume to calculate density.

1. What is a measurement?

A measurement describes a quantity using a number and a unit. For example, a mass of 35 g means 35 grams. The number alone does not tell a reader what was measured or how much of the unit was used.

QuantityWhat it describesCommon units
MassThe amount of matter in an objectkilogram (kg), gram (g), milligram (mg)
VolumeThe space an object occupiescubic centimeter (cm³), milliliter (mL), liter (L)

The SI base unit of mass is the kilogram; the SI derived unit for volume is the cubic meter (m³). Laboratory measurements often use grams, milliliters, and cubic centimeters because the objects are small.

Useful relationships: 1 kg = 1,000 g; 1 g = 1,000 mg; 1 L = 1,000 mL; and 1 mL = 1 cm³. These conversions are exact.

2. Mass: how much matter?

Mass describes the amount of matter in an object. Use a balance to measure mass. A sample with a mass of 42 g and another with a mass of 80 g are compared in the same unit: the second has greater mass.

Using a digital balance

  1. Place the balance on a stable, level surface and check that it reads zero.
  2. If a container is needed, place it on the balance and press tare to reset the display to zero.
  3. Add the sample and wait for the reading to stabilize. Record the displayed value and its unit.

Worked example · Finding sample mass

An empty container has a mass of 18.4 g. The container and sample together have a mass of 46.9 g. What is the mass of the sample?

Sample mass = 46.9 g − 18.4 g = 28.5 g

If the empty container had been tared before adding the sample, the balance would show the sample’s mass directly.

Mass and weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object, commonly measured in newtons (N). The same object has the same mass on Earth and on the Moon, while its weight differs because gravitational field strength differs. In everyday conversation, “weight” often refers to a mass reading in kilograms.

3. Volume: how much space?

Volume describes the space occupied by an object. Choose a method based on its shape and properties.

Rectangular solid

Measure its length, width, and height. Multiply the three dimensions using the same unit.

V = length × width × height

Irregular solid

For a suitable solid, measure how much the liquid level rises when the object is completely submerged.

V = final reading − initial reading

Worked example · Rectangular block

A block measures 4.0 cm long, 2.0 cm wide, and 3.0 cm high.

V = 4.0 cm × 2.0 cm × 3.0 cm = 24 cm³

Multiplying cm × cm × cm gives cm³. We will study how to choose the number of significant figures in Lesson 4.

Measuring an irregular solid by displacement

  1. Pour enough water into a graduated cylinder to cover the object. Record the initial reading.
  2. Gently lower the solid into the water. Make sure it is fully submerged and no air bubbles are trapped.
  3. Record the final reading. Subtract the initial volume from the final volume.

For water in a typical graduated cylinder, read the bottom of the curved surface, called the meniscus, at eye level. Record the reading using the instrument’s scale and the measurement conventions taught in class.

Worked example · Displacement

A pebble raises the water reading from 25.0 mL to 32.5 mL.

V = 32.5 mL − 25.0 mL = 7.5 mL = 7.5 cm³

The increase is the volume of the submerged pebble. The answer is not the final cylinder reading by itself.

Choose the liquid carefully. Water displacement is unsuitable if the object dissolves in water, reacts with it, or absorbs a significant amount. A floating object must be fully submerged using an appropriate method; account for any additional displaced volume caused by a holder. Avoid splashing and use safe, nonreactive materials.

A plastic cube is larger than a metal cube. Can you conclude that the plastic cube has more mass? Explain.

Show answer

No. A larger volume does not guarantee a greater mass. Measure each cube with a balance to compare their masses. Materials can have different densities, which we will investigate in the next lesson.

5. Key terms and takeaways

  • Measurement: a numerical value paired with a unit.
  • Mass: amount of matter; measured using a balance, commonly in grams.
  • Weight: gravitational force on an object; commonly expressed in newtons.
  • Volume: space occupied; for a rectangular solid, V = length × width × height.
  • Displacement: for an appropriate fully submerged object, its volume equals the final liquid reading minus the initial reading.
  • Unit relationship: 1 mL = 1 cm³.
