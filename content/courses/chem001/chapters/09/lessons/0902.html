Investigating the Properties of Hydrocarbons

Real-World Scenario

Every time a vehicle starts, a gas stove is ignited, or a candle burns, hydrocarbons are being used. Hydrocarbons serve as the primary components of fuels, plastics, lubricants, synthetic fibers, and thousands of industrial products. Understanding their structures and properties enables engineers and scientists to develop cleaner fuels, stronger materials, and more sustainable technologies.

Learning Objectives

  • Define hydrocarbons.
  • Differentiate saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.
  • Classify hydrocarbons into alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic hydrocarbons.
  • Describe the physical and chemical properties of hydrocarbons.
  • Relate hydrocarbons to fuels, petroleum products, and environmental issues.

What are Hydrocarbons?

Hydrocarbons are the simplest organic compounds because they contain only carbon (C) and hydrogen (H) atoms.

Hydrocarbons serve as the building blocks of many organic compounds.

Most fuels, plastics, waxes, lubricants, and many synthetic materials originate from hydrocarbons.

Carbon Skeletons

Carbon atoms can connect in different arrangements, producing molecules with different properties.

Carbon Skeleton Description
Straight Chain C—C—C—C
Branched Chain Main chain with side branches
Ring Structure Carbon atoms joined into a closed ring
Examples include:
  • n-Butane (straight chain)
  • Isobutane (branched chain)
  • Benzene (ring)

Saturated and Unsaturated Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are classified according to the types of bonds between carbon atoms.

Type Carbon Bonds Example
Saturated Only single bonds Methane
Unsaturated Contains double or triple bonds Ethene, Ethyne
Saturated hydrocarbons contain the maximum possible number of hydrogen atoms, while unsaturated hydrocarbons contain fewer hydrogen atoms because of multiple bonds.

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons containing only single covalent bonds.

Aromatic Hydrocarbons

Aromatic hydrocarbons contain one or more benzene rings with delocalized π electrons.

Aromatic compounds possess exceptional stability because of electron delocalization within the ring.
Compound Formula Common Use
Benzene C₆H₆ Industrial raw material
Toluene C₇H₈ Paint thinner
Xylene C₈H₁₀ Industrial solvent

Chemical Properties of Hydrocarbons

1. Combustion

Hydrocarbons burn in oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, water, and heat.

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
Combustion powers:
  • Vehicle engines
  • Power plants
  • Gas stoves

2. Addition Reactions

Alkenes and alkynes can undergo addition reactions because of their multiple bonds.

Ethene reacts with hydrogen:
C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆

Check Your Understanding

1. What elements are found in all hydrocarbons?

Carbon and hydrogen.

2. What is the difference between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons?

Saturated hydrocarbons contain only single bonds, while unsaturated hydrocarbons contain at least one double or triple bond.

3. Identify the hydrocarbon family of propane.

Alkane.

4. Which hydrocarbon family contains carbon-carbon double bonds?

Alkenes.

5. Which hydrocarbon family contains carbon-carbon triple bonds?

Alkynes.

6. Explain why hydrocarbons are generally insoluble in water.

Hydrocarbons are nonpolar, whereas water is polar. Because "like dissolves like," nonpolar hydrocarbons do not dissolve well in water.

7. Which hydrocarbon family includes benzene?

Aromatic hydrocarbons.

8. Write one environmental problem associated with burning hydrocarbons.

Burning hydrocarbons releases carbon dioxide, contributing to climate change, and incomplete combustion can produce toxic carbon monoxide.

9. Which hydrocarbon family is commonly used in plastic production?

Alkenes, particularly ethene, which is used to produce polyethylene.

10. Why do larger hydrocarbons generally have higher boiling points than smaller hydrocarbons?

Larger hydrocarbon molecules have stronger intermolecular (London dispersion) forces, requiring more energy to separate them during boiling.

Lesson Summary

  • Hydrocarbons are organic compounds composed only of carbon and hydrogen.
  • Carbon atoms can form straight chains, branched chains, and ring structures.
  • Hydrocarbons are classified into alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic hydrocarbons.
  • Most hydrocarbons are nonpolar, insoluble in water, and combustible.
  • Hydrocarbons are the primary components of petroleum and many fuels.
  • Hydrocarbons are essential in engineering, manufacturing, transportation, and energy production, but their use also presents environmental challenges.
