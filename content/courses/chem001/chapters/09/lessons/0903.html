Basic Functional Groups

Real-World Scenario

The scent of flowers, the flavor of fruits, the alcohol in hand sanitizers, the solvents used in laboratories, and many medicines all owe their unique properties to specific groups of atoms called functional groups. Instead of memorizing every organic compound, chemists identify these functional groups because they determine how molecules behave and react. Understanding functional groups allows chemists, engineers, pharmacists, and biologists to predict the properties and uses of millions of organic compounds.

Learning Objectives

  • Define a functional group.
  • Identify the most common functional groups found in organic compounds.
  • Recognize the general structure of each functional group.
  • Relate functional groups to the properties and uses of everyday substances.

What is a Functional Group?

A functional group is a specific atom or group of atoms attached to a carbon skeleton that determines the characteristic properties and chemical reactions of an organic compound.

Think of the carbon skeleton as the "framework" of the molecule, while the functional group acts like a "special feature" that determines how the molecule behaves.

Different molecules may have similar carbon skeletons but behave very differently because they contain different functional groups.

Alcohols (–OH)

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl group (–OH) attached to a carbon atom.

R—OH
The hydroxyl group makes alcohols more polar than hydrocarbons and allows many alcohols to dissolve in water.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Methanol CH₃OH Fuel, industrial solvent
Ethanol CH₃CH₂OH Alcoholic beverages, disinfectants, fuel
Isopropyl Alcohol C₃H₇OH Hand sanitizers and rubbing alcohol
Characteristics
  • Contain the hydroxyl (–OH) group.
  • Generally polar.
  • Can form hydrogen bonds.
  • Often have relatively high boiling points compared to hydrocarbons.

Ethers (–O–)

Ethers contain an oxygen atom bonded to two carbon atoms.

R—O—R'
Unlike alcohols, ethers do not contain an O–H bond, so they cannot form hydrogen bonds with one another.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Dimethyl Ether CH₃OCH₃ Aerosol propellant
Diethyl Ether C₂H₅OC₂H₅ Laboratory solvent
Methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) C₅H₁₂O Gasoline additive
Characteristics
  • Contain one oxygen atom between two carbon groups.
  • Generally less polar than alcohols.
  • Good organic solvents.
  • Usually have lower boiling points than comparable alcohols.

Aldehydes (–CHO)

Aldehydes contain a carbonyl group located at the end of a carbon chain.

R—CHO
The carbonyl carbon is always located at the end of the molecule.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Formaldehyde HCHO Preservative, resin production
Acetaldehyde CH₃CHO Chemical manufacturing
Vanillin C₈H₈O₃ Vanilla flavoring
Characteristics
  • Contain a terminal carbonyl group.
  • Generally reactive.
  • Often possess distinctive odors.
  • Important in fragrances and food flavorings.

Ketones (>C=O)

Ketones contain a carbonyl group bonded to two carbon atoms.

R—CO—R'
Unlike aldehydes, the carbonyl carbon of a ketone is located within the carbon chain rather than at the end.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Acetone CH₃COCH₃ Nail polish remover
Butanone (MEK) C₄H₈O Industrial solvent
Cyclohexanone C₆H₁₀O Nylon production
Characteristics
  • Contain an internal carbonyl group.
  • Excellent organic solvents.
  • Usually have pleasant or sweet odors.
  • Commonly used in paints, coatings, and plastics.

Carboxylic Acids (–COOH)

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group (–COOH), which consists of a carbonyl group (C=O) and a hydroxyl group (–OH) attached to the same carbon atom.

R—COOH
Carboxylic acids are often acidic because they can donate a hydrogen ion (H⁺).

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Formic Acid HCOOH Found in ant stings
Acetic Acid CH₃COOH Main acid in vinegar
Citric Acid C₆H₈O₇ Found in citrus fruits
Characteristics
  • Contain the –COOH group.
  • Can donate H⁺ ions.
  • Often have sour taste.
  • Common in food, medicine, and biological systems.

Esters (–COOR)

Esters contain a carbonyl group attached to an oxygen atom that is also bonded to another carbon group.

R—COO—R'
Many esters have pleasant fruity odors and are used in perfumes and flavorings.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Ethyl Acetate CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ Solvent, nail polish remover
Isoamyl Acetate C₇H₁₄O₂ Banana flavoring
Methyl Salicylate C₈H₈O₃ Wintergreen scent, pain-relief rubs
Characteristics
  • Contain the –COOR group.
  • Often have fruity or sweet odors.
  • Used in flavorings, fragrances, and solvents.
  • Formed from carboxylic acids and alcohols.

Amines (–NH₂)

Amines contain nitrogen bonded to carbon and hydrogen atoms. They are organic derivatives of ammonia.

R—NH₂
Amines are often basic and are common in medicines, dyes, and biological molecules.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Methylamine CH₃NH₂ Chemical manufacturing
Aniline C₆H₅NH₂ Dye production
Dopamine C₈H₁₁NO₂ Neurotransmitter
Characteristics
  • Contain nitrogen.
  • Often basic.
  • May have strong or fishy odors.
  • Important in pharmaceuticals and biological systems.

Amides (–CONH₂)

Amides contain a carbonyl group directly bonded to a nitrogen atom.

R—CONH₂
Amide bonds are especially important in proteins, where they connect amino acids together.

Examples

Compound Formula Common Use
Acetamide CH₃CONH₂ Organic synthesis
Urea CO(NH₂)₂ Fertilizers, skin-care products
Paracetamol C₈H₉NO₂ Pain reliever
Characteristics
  • Contain the –CONH₂ group.
  • Less basic than amines.
  • Common in medicines and biological molecules.
  • Amide linkages form the backbone of proteins.

How Functional Groups Affect Properties

Property Functional Group Influence
Polarity Oxygen- and nitrogen-containing groups often increase polarity.
Solubility Alcohols, acids, amines, and amides may dissolve well in water.
Boiling Point Groups capable of hydrogen bonding often raise boiling points.
Odor Esters often produce fruity smells; amines may smell fishy.
Acidity or Basicity Carboxylic acids are acidic; amines are basic.

Engineering, Medical, and Everyday Applications

Field Functional Group Importance
Pharmacy Functional groups affect drug activity and solubility.
Food Science Esters contribute to fruit flavors and aromas.
Materials Engineering Amides appear in nylon and protein-like polymers.
Environmental Science Functional groups influence pollutant solubility and persistence.
Biochemistry Alcohols, amines, acids, and amides appear in biomolecules.

Check Your Understanding

1. What functional group is found in alcohols?

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl group, –OH.

2. What functional group is present in carboxylic acids?

Carboxylic acids contain the –COOH group.

3. Which functional group is commonly associated with fruity odors?

Esters are commonly associated with fruity odors.

4. What is the difference between an aldehyde and a ketone?

An aldehyde has a terminal carbonyl group, while a ketone has a carbonyl group within the carbon chain.

5. Which functional group is basic and contains nitrogen?

Amines are basic nitrogen-containing functional groups.

6. Which functional group is important in proteins?

Amide groups are important because amide bonds connect amino acids in proteins.

7. Identify the functional group in CH₃CH₂OH.

CH₃CH₂OH contains an alcohol functional group.

8. Identify the functional group in CH₃COOH.

CH₃COOH contains a carboxylic acid functional group.

9. Identify the functional group in CH₃COCH₃.

CH₃COCH₃ contains a ketone functional group.

10. Why do functional groups strongly affect organic compound properties?

Functional groups control polarity, solubility, boiling point, acidity, basicity, odor, and chemical reactivity.

Lesson Summary

  • Functional groups are specific groups of atoms that determine the behavior of organic compounds.
  • Alcohols contain –OH and often form hydrogen bonds.
  • Ethers contain –O– and are useful organic solvents.
  • Aldehydes contain terminal carbonyl groups, while ketones contain internal carbonyl groups.
  • Carboxylic acids contain –COOH and can donate H⁺ ions.
  • Esters often have pleasant fruity odors and are used in fragrances and flavorings.
  • Amines contain nitrogen and are often basic.
  • Amides are important in proteins, medicines, and polymers.
  • Recognizing functional groups helps predict the properties, uses, and reactivity of organic compounds.
