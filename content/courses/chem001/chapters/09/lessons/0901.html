Comparing Organic and Inorganic Compounds

Real-World Scenario

Every day you encounter thousands of chemical compounds. The gasoline that powers vehicles, the sugar in your food, the plastic bottle you drink from, the medicines you take, and even the proteins in your body are all examples of organic compounds. Meanwhile, table salt, water, limestone, metals, and many minerals are examples of inorganic compounds. Although both are composed of atoms, their structures and properties are very different. Understanding these differences helps chemists, engineers, pharmacists, and environmental scientists choose the right materials for different applications.

What is Organic Chemistry?

Organic Chemistry is the branch of chemistry that studies compounds primarily composed of carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen and often to oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, and halogens.

Organic Chemistry focuses mainly on compounds containing carbon-carbon (C—C) and carbon-hydrogen (C—H) bonds.

Today, millions of organic compounds are known, making Organic Chemistry one of the largest branches of chemistry.

A Brief History of Organic Chemistry

Early scientists believed that compounds produced by living organisms possessed a special "vital force" that could not be reproduced in the laboratory.

Vital Force Theory

Before the 1800s, scientists believed:

  • Plants and animals could produce organic compounds.
  • Laboratories could only produce inorganic compounds.

This experiment disproved the Vital Force Theory and demonstrated that organic compounds could be synthesized in laboratories.

Why is Carbon Unique?

Carbon is often called the element of life because it forms the backbone of nearly all biological molecules.

Carbon can form four covalent bonds simultaneously, allowing it to build an enormous variety of stable molecules.
Unique Property Importance
Four valence electrons Forms four covalent bonds
Catenation Carbon bonds with itself to form long chains and rings
Multiple bonding Forms single, double, and triple bonds
Strong covalent bonds Produces stable compounds
Versatility Allows millions of different compounds to exist

Carbon Skeletons

Carbon atoms may connect together in different ways, producing many different molecular structures.

Organic vs Inorganic Compounds

Although both contain atoms and chemical bonds, they differ in composition and properties.

Typical Bonding Covalent Ionic or Covalent
Contains C—H Bond Usually Yes Usually No

Electrical Conductivity Poor conductor Some conduct electricity
Flammability Often flammable Usually nonflammable

Important Note

Not every carbon-containing compound is considered organic.

Several carbon-containing compounds are classified as inorganic, including:

Compound Classification
CO₂ Inorganic
CO Inorganic
CaCO₃ Inorganic
NaHCO₃ Inorganic

These compounds do not possess the characteristic carbon-hydrogen (C—H) framework found in most organic compounds.

Check Your Understanding

1. What is Organic Chemistry?

Organic Chemistry is the study of compounds primarily containing carbon bonded to hydrogen and other elements.

2. Why is carbon considered unique?

Because it forms four covalent bonds, bonds with itself (catenation), and creates millions of stable compounds.

3. Which scientist disproved the Vital Force Theory?

Friedrich Wöhler in 1828 through the synthesis of urea.

4. Which compound is organic: glucose or sodium chloride?

Glucose is organic, while sodium chloride is inorganic.

5. Name two carbon-containing compounds that are classified as inorganic.

Examples include CO₂, CO, CaCO₃, and NaHCO₃.

6. Compare one physical property of organic compounds with inorganic compounds.

Organic compounds generally have lower melting and boiling points, while inorganic compounds often have higher melting and boiling points.

7. Why are most organic compounds poor conductors of electricity?

Most organic compounds consist of neutral covalent molecules and lack freely moving charged particles.

8. Which characteristic of carbon allows it to form long chains and rings?

Catenation, the ability of carbon atoms to bond with one another.

9. Why is polyethylene considered an organic compound?

It is composed primarily of carbon and hydrogen atoms connected by covalent bonds.

10. Explain why calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) is classified as inorganic even though it contains carbon.

Calcium carbonate does not contain carbon-hydrogen (C—H) bonds, which are characteristic of most organic compounds.

Lesson Summary

  • Organic Chemistry studies compounds primarily containing carbon and hydrogen.
  • The Vital Force Theory was disproved by Friedrich Wöhler's synthesis of urea.
  • Carbon is unique because it forms four covalent bonds and exhibits catenation.
  • Organic and inorganic compounds differ in composition, bonding, and physical properties.
  • Not all carbon-containing compounds are organic; compounds such as CO₂ and CaCO₃ are classified as inorganic.
  • Organic compounds are found in fuels, plastics, medicines, and living organisms, while inorganic compounds are essential in construction, agriculture, industry, and biological systems.
