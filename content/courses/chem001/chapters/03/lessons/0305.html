Molar Mass and Percent Composition

Real-World Scenario

A food manufacturer wants to determine how much calcium is present in a nutritional supplement. A pharmaceutical company must know the exact percentage of each element in a medicine to ensure proper dosage and effectiveness. Chemists use molar mass and percent composition to determine the quantitative composition of substances and compare different compounds.

Learning Objectives

  • Define molar mass and percent composition.
  • Calculate the molar mass of elements and compounds.
  • Determine the mass contribution of each element in a compound.
  • Calculate percent composition from a chemical formula.
  • Apply percent composition in chemical analysis.

What is Molar Mass?

The molar mass of a substance is the mass of one mole of that substance. It serves as the bridge between the microscopic world of atoms and molecules and the measurable world of grams.

Molar Mass = Mass of 1 mole of a substance

Unit: g/mol

The numerical value of an element's molar mass is usually the same as its atomic mass from the periodic table, but the unit changes from amu to g/mol.

Examples

  • Carbon (C) = 12.01 g/mol
  • Hydrogen (H) = 1.01 g/mol
  • Oxygen (O) = 16.00 g/mol
  • Sodium (Na) = 22.99 g/mol

Calculating Molar Mass of Compounds

For compounds, the molar mass is obtained by adding the molar masses of all atoms present in the chemical formula.

Always multiply the atomic mass by the number of atoms indicated by the subscript before adding.

Worked Example 1: Water (H₂O)

Calculate the molar mass of water.

Step 1: Identify the elements.

  • Hydrogen (H) = 1.01 g/mol
  • Oxygen (O) = 16.00 g/mol

Step 2: Multiply by subscripts.

H = 2 × 1.01 = 2.02
O = 1 × 16.00 = 16.00

Step 3: Add contributions.

2.02 + 16.00 = 18.02 g/mol

Molar Mass of H₂O = 18.02 g/mol

Worked Example 2: Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)

Step 1: Atomic masses

  • C = 12.01 g/mol
  • O = 16.00 g/mol

Step 2: Apply subscripts

C = 12.01
O = 2 × 16.00 = 32.00

Step 3: Add

12.01 + 32.00 = 44.01 g/mol

Molar Mass of CO₂ = 44.01 g/mol

What is Percent Composition?

Percent composition describes the percentage by mass of each element in a compound.

It tells us how much of a compound's total mass comes from each element.

Percent composition is useful for:
  • Identifying unknown substances
  • Checking purity of compounds
  • Determining nutrient content
  • Verifying chemical formulas

Formula for Percent Composition

Percent Composition = (Element Mass in 1 mole ÷ Molar Mass of Compound) × 100

Worked Example 3: Percent Composition of Water

Determine the percent composition of hydrogen and oxygen in H₂O.

Step 1: Determine element masses.

Hydrogen = 2.02 g
Oxygen = 16.00 g

Step 2: Determine molar mass.

18.02 g/mol

Step 3: Calculate percentages.

% H = (2.02 ÷ 18.02) × 100 = 11.21%

% O = (16.00 ÷ 18.02) × 100 = 88.79%

Notice that the percentages add to approximately 100%.

Worked Example 4: Percent Composition of CO₂

Step 1: Molar mass of CO₂

44.01 g/mol

Step 2: Carbon contribution

(12.01 ÷ 44.01) × 100 = 27.29%

Step 3: Oxygen contribution

(32.00 ÷ 44.01) × 100 = 72.71%

Interpreting Percent Composition

Percent composition allows chemists to compare substances and understand their makeup.

Example

Water contains approximately:

  • 11% hydrogen by mass
  • 89% oxygen by mass

Even though water contains twice as many hydrogen atoms as oxygen atoms, oxygen contributes more mass because oxygen atoms are much heavier than hydrogen atoms.

The number of atoms does not necessarily determine the percentage by mass. Atomic mass must also be considered.

Applications of Percent Composition

Field Application
Nutrition Determining nutrient content in foods
Pharmaceuticals Analyzing medicine composition
Environmental Science Analyzing pollutants and contaminants
Materials Science Determining alloy composition

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply atomic masses by subscripts.
  • Using atomic number instead of atomic mass.
  • Not calculating the total molar mass before determining percentages.
  • Rounding too early during calculations.
  • Assuming more atoms always means greater percent composition.

Check Your Understanding

14.01 + 3.03 = 17.04 g/mol

2. Calculate the molar mass of CH₄.

12.01 + (4 × 1.01) = 16.05 g/mol

Oxygen contributes more mass because:
32.00 g > 12.01 g

4. Why do percent compositions usually add up to approximately 100%?

Because all elements present in the compound account for the entire mass of the compound.

Lesson Summary

  • Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.
  • For compounds, molar mass is found by adding the masses of all atoms in the formula.
  • Percent composition describes the percentage by mass of each element in a compound.
  • Percent composition calculations require the molar mass of the compound.
  • Atomic mass influences percent composition more than the number of atoms alone.
  • Molar mass and percent composition are essential tools for chemical analysis and formula determination.
