Isotopes and Isotope Notation

Carbon-12, carbon-13, and carbon-14 are all carbon. Use isotope notation to see what stays the same and what changes.

ObjectivesIsotopesNotationWriting notationPracticeTakeaways

Learning objectives

At the end of the lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain why isotopes belong to the same element despite having different neutron counts.
  • Interpret isotope notation and hyphenated isotope names.
  • Calculate protons, neutrons, electrons, and mass number for a specified isotope.
  • Construct correct isotope notation from particle counts and identify isotope pairs.
Connect to Lesson 3: Atomic number Z equals protons, mass number A equals protons plus neutrons, and a neutral atom has electrons equal to protons.

1. Same element, different neutron counts

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. They have the same number of protons and different mass numbers.

Neutral atom Protons Neutrons Electrons Mass number
Carbon-12 6 6 6 12
Carbon-13 6 7 6 13
Carbon-14 6 8 6 14

All three have Z = 6, so they are carbon. Their neutron counts and mass numbers differ. Some isotopes are stable and some radioactive; being an isotope does not automatically mean an atom is radioactive.

Think it through

Atom A has 8 protons and 8 neutrons. Atom B has 8 protons and 10 neutrons. Are they isotopes?

Show answer

Yes. Both have 8 protons (oxygen), but their neutron counts differ. Their mass numbers are 16 and 18.

2. Read isotope notation

In nuclear or isotope notation, the element symbol is written with mass number A at the upper left and atomic number Z at the lower left.

146C

Part Meaning For carbon-14
Upper left: A = 14 Protons + neutrons 14 nucleons
Lower left: Z = 6 Protons 6 protons
Symbol: C Element identity Carbon

Neutrons = A − Z = 14 − 6 = 8. With no charge shown, this represents a neutral atom, so it has 6 electrons.

Carbon-14 and C-14 are hyphenated names: the number is the mass number. Find its atomic number from the periodic table when needed. A periodic-table average is not the mass number of each individual atom.

Worked example · Interpret sodium-23

For 2311Na, A = 23 and Z = 11. There are 11 protons, 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons, and, because no charge is shown, 11 electrons.

3. Construct isotope notation

  1. Count the protons; use Z to find the element symbol on the periodic table.
  2. Add protons and neutrons to obtain A.
  3. Write A at the upper left and Z at the lower left of the symbol.
  4. If a charge is specified, write it at the upper right. Otherwise, use a neutral atom when counting electrons.

Worked example · From particles to notation

An atom has 8 protons, 10 neutrons, and 8 electrons. Z = 8 identifies oxygen, O. A = 8 + 10 = 18. Equal protons and electrons mean it is neutral.

188O   or oxygen-18

Extension · When a charge is shown

3517Cl− has 17 protons and 18 neutrons. The −1 charge means it has gained one electron, giving 18 electrons. Its isotope is chlorine-35 whether the chlorine atom is neutral or is a chloride ion.

Changing electrons forms an ion. Changing neutrons gives a different isotope of the same element. Changing protons gives a different element.

Challenge · Explain your reasoning

Two neutral atoms contain 17 protons each. One has 18 neutrons and the other has 20. Name both isotopes and explain their relationship.

Show a possible answer

Both are chlorine because Z = 17. Their mass numbers are 35 and 37, so they are chlorine-35 and chlorine-37. They are isotopes because they have the same proton count and different neutron counts.

6. Key terms and takeaways

  • Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
  • In isotope notation, A is at the upper left and Z is at the lower left of the element symbol.
  • Protons = Z; neutrons = A − Z; for neutral atoms electrons = protons.
  • In a hyphenated name such as oxygen-18, the number is the mass number.
