Relative Atomic Mass and Average Atomic Mass

Chlorine has isotopes with different masses. Why, then, does a periodic table give a single value near 35.45? Explore the weighted average behind that value.

ObjectivesMass termsWeighted averageWorked examplesApplicationsPractice

Learning objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Distinguish mass number, isotopic mass, average atomic mass, and relative atomic mass.
  • Explain the carbon-12 reference and convert isotope percentages to fractions.
  • Calculate an abundance-weighted average from isotopic data and assess its reasonableness.
  • Explain why isotopic composition matters in periodic-table values and chemical calculations.
Connect to Lesson 4: Isotopes have the same number of protons but different neutron counts. Their different masses and abundances together determine a sample’s average atomic mass.

1. Four related mass terms

Term What it means Example
Mass number, A Whole-number count of protons and neutrons in one nuclide. Chlorine-35 has A = 35.
Isotopic mass Mass of one atom of a specified isotope, commonly expressed in unified atomic mass units, u. A chlorine-35 atom has mass approximately 34.969 u.
Average atomic mass Average mass per atom for an element in a specified isotopic mixture, weighted by isotope abundance; expressed in u. A typical chlorine mixture has an average near 35.45 u per atom.
Relative atomic mass, Ar The ratio of the average atomic mass to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom; it has no unit. For a sample averaging 35.45 u, Ar = 35.45.

By definition, 1 u is one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom in its ground state. Relative atomic mass and average atomic mass are closely linked: the former is dimensionless and, for the same sample, has the same numerical value as the latter expressed in u.

Ar = average mass per atom ÷ 1 u

Both concepts can depend on isotope abundance. Relative atomic mass is also an abundance-weighted comparison; it is not based solely on one isotope when an element is present as a mixture.

Try the distinction

A sample has an average atomic mass of 24.3 u per atom. What is its relative atomic mass?

Show answer

24.3, without a unit. It is the ratio of 24.3 u to 1 u for the same sample.

2. Why the average is weighted

In an isotope mixture, the fractional abundance is the fraction of the element’s atoms that belong to a given isotope. Isotopes that occur more often contribute more to the average.

Illustration only; circles do not represent actual atomic size.

If the masses are 10.0 u and 11.0 u, the average is (10.0 × 0.80) + (11.0 × 0.20) = 10.2 u. It is closer to 10.0 u because the lighter isotope is more abundant. An equal, unweighted mean would incorrectly treat both isotopes as equally common.

average atomic mass = Σ (isotopic mass × fractional abundance)

  1. Convert each percent to a decimal: abundance fraction = percent ÷ 100.
  2. Check that all fractions add to 1, allowing for rounding.
  3. Multiply each isotopic mass by its fraction, then add the contributions.
  4. Report the mass in u and check that the result lies between the isotope masses.
A decimal atomic mass is often due to a mixture of isotopes, but isotope masses themselves also need not be whole numbers. An average is not the mass of every individual atom.

3. Worked examples

Example 1 · Two isotopes

A fictional element has isotope masses of 24.00 u (70.0%) and 26.00 u (30.0%). Find the average atomic mass.

Fractions: 0.700 and 0.300; their sum is 1.000.

Average = (24.00 u × 0.700) + (26.00 u × 0.300)
= 16.800 u + 7.800 u = 24.6 u.

Check: 24.6 is between 24.00 and 26.00 and is closer to 24.00, the more abundant isotope. The relative atomic mass for this sample is 24.6, without a unit.

Example 2 · Three isotopes

A sample contains 23.985 u at 79.0%, 24.986 u at 10.0%, and 25.983 u at 11.0%.

Average = (23.985 × 0.790) + (24.986 × 0.100) + (25.983 × 0.110)
= 18.94815 + 2.4986 + 2.85813 = 24.30488 u ≈ 24.3 u.

Keep extra digits during the calculation and round the final answer according to the precision of the data. The average is closer to the most abundant isotope.

Example 3 · Estimate using mass numbers

Suppose a sample is 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37, and measured isotope masses are not supplied. Estimate the average using 35 u and 37 u.

(35 × 0.75) + (37 × 0.25) = 35.5 u, an estimate for this simplified mixture. Actual isotope masses are not exactly their mass numbers, and natural abundances may vary.

Try it first

A sample has isotopes of 20.0 u and 22.0 u, each at 50.0%. Find its average atomic mass.

Show answer

(20.0 × 0.500) + (22.0 × 0.500) = 21.0 u.

4. Find a missing abundance

Example · Two-isotope mixture

A sample has isotopes with masses 10.0 u and 12.0 u. Its average mass is 10.5 u. Estimate the percentage of the heavier isotope.

Let x be the heavier isotope’s fraction; the lighter isotope’s fraction is 1 − x.

10.5 = 10.0(1 − x) + 12.0x
10.5 = 10.0 + 2.0x
x = 0.25 = 25%. The lighter isotope is 75%.

Check: 10.0(0.75) + 12.0(0.25) = 10.5 u. The answer is an estimate because the supplied values are rounded.

Avoid these errors: Multiply by 0.75, not 75; add all isotope contributions; distinguish an average from a mass number; and do not assume all isotopes are equally abundant. The average must fall between the smallest and largest contributing masses.

5. Why atomic mass matters

Periodic-table atomic weights help chemists compare elements and calculate formula masses and molar masses. For example, using a typical relative atomic mass of 12.01 for carbon, carbon’s molar mass is approximately 12.01 g/mol. Isotopic composition matters when greater precision is needed.

A standard atomic weight represents an element in specified normal terrestrial materials. Natural isotopic composition can vary, and some standard atomic weights are reported as intervals. Some elements have no standard atomic weight because a characteristic terrestrial isotope composition cannot be defined. Challenge · Explain a result

A two-isotope sample contains 90.0% of an isotope with mass 28.0 u and 10.0% of an isotope with mass 30.0 u. Calculate the average and explain why an equal average would be misleading.

Show a possible answer

(28.0 × 0.900) + (30.0 × 0.100) = 28.2 u. An equal mean would give 29.0 u, but it would ignore the much greater abundance of the 28.0 u isotope.

7. Key takeaways

  • Mass number is a nucleon count, while isotope mass is a measured mass.
  • Average atomic mass weights isotopic masses by their abundance in the sample.
  • Relative atomic mass compares the average atomic mass with 1 u; it is dimensionless.
  • For the same mixture, average mass in u and relative atomic mass have the same numerical value.
  • Use fractional abundances that add to 1 and check that the result lies between contributing isotope masses.
