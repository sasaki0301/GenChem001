Chapter Summary: Periodic Table and Trends

Chapter Big Idea

The periodic table is more than a list of elements—it is a powerful predictive tool. By understanding the organization of the periodic table and the trends that occur across periods and down groups, chemists can predict atomic size, ion formation, reactivity, bonding behavior, and many other properties without performing experiments.

1. The Periodic Table as a Predictive Tool

The periodic table organizes elements according to increasing atomic number and recurring chemical properties.

Using the periodic table, we can predict:
  • Number of protons, neutrons, and electrons
  • Valence electrons
  • Common ionic charges
  • Metallic and nonmetallic behavior
  • Atomic size
  • Reactivity
  • Bonding tendencies

2. Structure of the Periodic Table

Feature Description
Period Horizontal row
Group (Family) Vertical column
Atomic Number Number of protons
Atomic Mass Weighted average mass of isotopes
Elements within the same group generally possess similar chemical properties because they have the same number of valence electrons.

3. Metals, Nonmetals, and Metalloids

Category Characteristics
Metals Conductive, malleable, ductile, form positive ions
Nonmetals Poor conductors, brittle, form negative ions
Metalloids Intermediate properties, semiconductors

Examples

  • Metals: Na, Mg, Fe, Cu
  • Nonmetals: O, N, Cl, S
  • Metalloids: B, Si, Ge, As

5. Atomic Structure from the Periodic Table

Atomic Number = Number of Protons

Number of Electrons = Number of Protons (neutral atom)

Neutrons = Mass Number − Atomic Number

Example: Oxygen

  • Atomic Number = 8
  • Mass Number ≈ 16
  • Protons = 8
  • Electrons = 8
  • Neutrons = 8

Valence electrons largely determine bonding behavior and chemical reactivity.

7. Predicting Common Ions

Examples

Na → Na⁺

Mg → Mg²⁺

Cl → Cl⁻

O → O²⁻

Atoms tend to gain or lose electrons to achieve stable noble-gas electron configurations.

8. Why Periodic Trends Exist

Periodic trends are controlled by:
  • Effective Nuclear Charge
  • Electron Shielding
  • Distance of Valence Electrons from the Nucleus

As atomic number increases, these factors influence atomic properties in predictable ways.

9. Atomic Radius

Atomic radius measures atomic size.

Direction Trend
Across a Period → Decreases
Down a Group ↓ Increases

Example

Na > Mg > Al > Si > P > S > Cl

Atomic radius decreases from left to right across Period 3.

10. Ionic Radius

Ion Type Size Change
Cation Smaller than parent atom
Anion Larger than parent atom
Na⁺ < Na

Cl⁻ > Cl

11. Ionization Energy

Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom.

Direction Trend
Across a Period → Increases
Down a Group ↓ Decreases
Atoms with smaller radii generally have higher ionization energies.

12. Electron Affinity

Electron affinity measures an atom's tendency to gain electrons.

Direction General Trend
Across a Period → More Negative
Down a Group ↓ Less Negative
Halogens possess some of the highest electron affinities.

13. Electronegativity

Electronegativity measures an atom's ability to attract bonding electrons.

Direction Trend
Across a Period → Increases
Down a Group ↓ Decreases

Most Electronegative Element

Fluorine (F)

14. Metallic Character

Metallic character describes how readily an atom loses electrons.

Direction Trend
Across a Period → Decreases
Down a Group ↓ Increases
Most Metallic Region:

Bottom Left of the Periodic Table

Least Metallic Region:

Upper Right of the Periodic Table

15. Summary of Major Periodic Trends

Property Across a Period → Down a Group ↓
Atomic Radius Decreases Increases
Ionization Energy Increases Decreases
Electron Affinity More Negative Less Negative
Electronegativity Increases Decreases
Metallic Character Decreases Increases

16. Using Trends to Predict Reactivity

Alkali Metals

Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs

Reactivity increases down the group.

Halogens

F > Cl > Br > I

Reactivity decreases down the group.

Chapter Mastery Check

1. How many valence electrons does phosphorus have?

5 valence electrons.

2. Which element has the larger atomic radius: Li or K?

K has the larger atomic radius.

3. Which element has higher electronegativity: Na or Cl?

Cl has higher electronegativity.

4. What ion does magnesium commonly form?

Mg²⁺

5. Why are noble gases generally unreactive?

They possess complete valence shells.

6. Which property generally increases across a period: atomic radius or ionization energy?

Ionization energy.

7. Which ion is larger: O or O²⁻?

O²⁻ is larger.

8. Which family contains the most reactive nonmetals?

The Halogens (Group 17).

Chapter Summary

  • The periodic table organizes elements according to atomic number and recurring chemical properties.
  • Groups contain elements with similar valence electron configurations and chemical behavior.
  • Metals tend to lose electrons, while nonmetals tend to gain electrons.
  • Valence electrons largely determine bonding and reactivity.
  • Atomic radius decreases across periods and increases down groups.
  • Ionization energy and electronegativity generally increase across periods.
  • Metallic character increases down groups and toward the lower-left region of the periodic table.
  • Periodic trends allow scientists to predict atomic behavior, bonding tendencies, and reactivity.
  • The periodic table remains one of chemistry's most powerful predictive tools.
