Chapter Summary: Chemical Bonding

Chapter Big Idea

Chemical bonding explains how atoms combine to form compounds. By understanding electron transfer, electron sharing, Lewis structures, molecular geometry, and molecular polarity, we can predict the properties and behavior of substances used in engineering, medicine, biology, and everyday life. Throughout this chapter, we learned not only how atoms bond, but also how molecular structure determines chemical behavior.

1. Why Atoms Form Chemical Bonds

Most atoms are unstable because they do not have complete valence shells. They become more stable by gaining, losing, or sharing electrons to achieve a noble gas electron configuration.

Atoms form chemical bonds to achieve a lower-energy, more stable electron configuration.

2. The Octet Rule

The Octet Rule states that atoms tend to gain, lose, or share electrons until they possess eight valence electrons.

Element Typical Behavior
Group 1 Lose 1 electron
Group 2 Lose 2 electrons
Group 16 Gain 2 electrons
Group 17 Gain 1 electron
Group 18 Already stable

10. VSEPR Theory

VSEPR Theory states that electron pairs repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible.

Electron-pair repulsion determines molecular geometry.

Chapter Mastery Challenge

1. Classify AlBr₃ as ionic or covalent based on the types of elements involved.

Although aluminum is a metal, AlBr₃ exhibits significant covalent character due to polarization. At this level, it is commonly classified as an ionic compound using the metal–nonmetal rule, but it is a good example of a compound that bridges both concepts.

2. Draw the Lewis structure of HCN and identify its molecular geometry.

Lewis Structure:
H—C≡N
Geometry around carbon: Linear.

3. Which molecule is polar: SO₃ or SO₂? Explain.

SO₂ is polar because its bent geometry prevents bond dipoles from canceling. SO₃ is nonpolar because it has a symmetrical trigonal planar geometry.

4. Explain why CCl₄ is nonpolar even though each C–Cl bond is polar.

CCl₄ has a symmetrical tetrahedral geometry. The bond dipoles cancel one another, producing no overall molecular dipole.

5. A chemist wants to dissolve an ionic compound efficiently. Which solvent is generally the best choice, water or hexane? Explain.

Water is the better solvent because it is polar and can stabilize positive and negative ions through ion–dipole interactions. Hexane is nonpolar and cannot effectively dissolve most ionic compounds.

Chapter Summary

  • Atoms form chemical bonds to achieve stable electron configurations.
  • Ionic bonds involve electron transfer, while covalent bonds involve electron sharing.
  • Lewis structures represent bonding pairs and lone pairs.
  • Most atoms follow the Octet Rule, with a few important exceptions.
  • VSEPR Theory predicts molecular geometry from electron-pair repulsion.
  • Bond polarity depends on electronegativity differences.
  • Molecular polarity depends on both bond polarity and molecular geometry.
  • Understanding bonding allows scientists and engineers to predict the physical and chemical properties of substances.
