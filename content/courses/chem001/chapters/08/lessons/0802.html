Lewis Structures and VSEPR Theory

Real-World Scenario

Why is water bent while carbon dioxide is linear? Why is methane tetrahedral? Why do some molecules have lone pairs while others do not? Chemists answer these questions by drawing Lewis structures and applying the Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) Theory. These models help us visualize how atoms are connected, where electrons are located, and what shapes molecules take.

Learning Objectives

  • Construct Lewis dot symbols.
  • Draw Lewis structures of simple molecules.
  • Identify bonding pairs and lone pairs.
  • Apply the Octet Rule while drawing molecules.
  • Recognize single, double, and triple bonds.
  • Predict molecular geometry using VSEPR Theory.

Lewis Dot Symbols

Lewis dot symbols represent only the valence electrons of an atom.

Each dot corresponds to one valence electron.

Lewis symbols allow chemists to quickly determine how atoms are likely to bond.

What is a Lewis Structure?

A Lewis structure is a diagram that shows:

  • How atoms are connected.
  • Shared electron pairs (chemical bonds).
  • Lone pairs of electrons.
Lewis structures show both bonding electrons and nonbonding (lone) electrons.

Bonding Pairs and Lone Pairs

Electron Pair Description
Bonding Pair Shared between two atoms to form a chemical bond.
Lone Pair Not shared; belongs to only one atom.

Water (H₂O)

Oxygen contains:

  • 2 bonding pairs
  • 2 lone pairs

How to Draw Lewis Structures

Follow these five steps for most covalent compounds.

Step 1 — Count the total number of valence electrons.

Step 2 — Select the central atom (usually the least electronegative atom, excluding hydrogen).

Step 3 — Connect surrounding atoms with single bonds.

Step 4 — Complete octets using lone pairs.

Step 5 — If necessary, form double or triple bonds until all atoms satisfy the octet rule.

Tips for Drawing Lewis Structures

  • Hydrogen is always a terminal atom.
  • Carbon is usually the central atom.
  • Halogens usually form one bond.
  • Oxygen usually forms two bonds.
  • Nitrogen usually forms three bonds.
  • Carbon usually forms four bonds.

Exceptions to the Octet Rule

Although the Octet Rule is useful for most main-group elements, some atoms do not always have eight valence electrons.

Exception Description Example
Incomplete Octet Some atoms are stable with fewer than eight electrons. BeCl₂, BF₃
Expanded Octet Elements in Period 3 and beyond may accommodate more than eight electrons. PCl₅, SF₆
Duet Rule Hydrogen becomes stable with only two electrons. H₂
For this course, most molecules you encounter will obey the Octet Rule. The exceptions introduced here prepare you for more advanced chemistry.

Example: Boron Trifluoride (BF₃)

Introduction to VSEPR Theory

Once a Lewis structure has been drawn, the next step is to determine the molecule's three-dimensional shape.

The Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) Theory states that electron pairs around a central atom repel one another and arrange themselves as far apart as possible.

Electron pairs repel each other.

The molecular shape minimizes these repulsive forces.

Electron Groups

In VSEPR Theory, each of the following counts as one electron group:

  • Single bond
  • Double bond
  • Triple bond
  • Lone pair
Water (H₂O)
  • 2 bonding pairs
  • 2 lone pairs

Total electron groups = 4

Check Your Understanding

1. What does a Lewis structure show?

It shows the arrangement of atoms, bonding pairs, and lone pairs of electrons.

2. How many valence electrons does carbon contribute when drawing Lewis structures?

Four valence electrons.

3. Which molecule contains a double bond?

Carbon dioxide (CO₂).

4. Which molecule contains a triple bond?

Nitrogen (N₂).

5. What is the molecular geometry of methane?

Tetrahedral.

6. What molecular geometry does ammonia exhibit?

Trigonal pyramidal.

7. Why is water bent instead of linear?

Because two lone pairs on oxygen repel the bonding pairs, changing the molecular shape.

8. Which molecule has a linear geometry?

Carbon dioxide (CO₂).

9. How many electron groups surround the carbon atom in methane?

Four electron groups.

10. Which VSEPR shape has an ideal bond angle of 109.5°?

Tetrahedral.

11. Why is BF₃ considered an exception to the Octet Rule?

Because boron has only six electrons surrounding it instead of eight.

12. Explain the basic idea behind VSEPR Theory.

Electron pairs repel one another and arrange themselves as far apart as possible, determining the molecular geometry.

Lesson Summary

  • Lewis dot symbols represent an atom's valence electrons.
  • Lewis structures illustrate bonding pairs and lone pairs.
  • Most atoms follow the Octet Rule, although some important exceptions exist.
  • Single, double, and triple bonds differ in the number of shared electron pairs.
  • VSEPR Theory predicts molecular geometry based on electron-pair repulsion.
  • Common molecular shapes include linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal pyramidal, and bent.
  • Molecular geometry influences many physical and chemical properties and forms the basis for understanding molecular polarity.
