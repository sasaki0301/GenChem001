The Combined Gas Law and Ideal Gas Law

Real-World Scenario

A scuba diver descends into deeper water and experiences increasing pressure. A weather balloon rises into the atmosphere where both temperature and pressure change. Engineers designing compressed gas systems must predict how gases behave when multiple variables change simultaneously. Individual gas laws describe relationships between two variables at a time. However, real situations often involve several changing variables. The Combined Gas Law and Ideal Gas Law allow scientists and engineers to analyze these more complex situations.

Law Variables Relationship
Boyle's Law Pressure and Volume Inverse

Gay-Lussac's Law Pressure and Temperature Direct
Avogadro's Law Volume and Moles Direct

The Combined Gas Law merges Boyle's, Charles's, and Gay-Lussac's Laws into a single equation.

The Combined Gas Law

The Combined Gas Law relates pressure, volume, and temperature when the amount of gas remains constant.

P₁V₁ T₁ = P₂V₂ T₂
Use the Combined Gas Law when pressure, volume, and temperature change but the number of moles remains constant.

Worked Example 1: Combined Gas Law

A gas occupies 2.00 L at 1.00 atm and 300 K. What volume will it occupy at 2.00 atm and 450 K?

Given:

  • P₁ = 1.00 atm
  • V₁ = 2.00 L
  • T₁ = 300 K
  • P₂ = 2.00 atm
  • T₂ = 450 K

Rearrange for V₂:

V₂ = P₁V₁T₂ P₂T₁
V₂ = (1.00)(2.00)(450) (2.00)(300) = 1.50 L

The Ideal Gas Law

The Ideal Gas Law combines all four gas variables into one equation.

PV = nRT
Symbol Meaning
P Pressure
V Volume
n Number of moles
R Gas constant
T Temperature (Kelvin)

The Gas Constant (R)

The value of R depends on the pressure units used.

Value of R Pressure Unit
0.0821 L·atm/mol·K atm
8.314 L·kPa/mol·K kPa
For most General Chemistry problems, use:

R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K

Worked Example 2: Solving for Pressure

Calculate the pressure of 1.50 mol of gas occupying 10.0 L at 300 K.

PV = nRT

Rearrange for P:

P = nRT V
P = (1.50)(0.0821)(300) 10.0 = 3.69 atm

Worked Example 3: Solving for Volume

What volume will 2.00 mol of gas occupy at 1.00 atm and 273 K?

Rearrange for V:

V = nRT P
V = (2.00)(0.0821)(273) 1.00 = 44.8 L

Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP)

STP provides a standard reference condition for gases.

Variable Value
Temperature 273 K (0°C)
Pressure 1 atm
At STP, 1 mole of an ideal gas occupies approximately 22.4 L.

Molar Volume at STP

1 mol gas = 22.4 L

Example

How many liters will 3.00 mol of gas occupy at STP?

3.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol = 67.2 L

When Should Each Gas Law Be Used?

Situation Law Used
P and V change Boyle's Law
V and T change Charles's Law
P and T change Gay-Lussac's Law
V and n change Avogadro's Law
P, V, and T change Combined Gas Law
P, V, T, and n involved Ideal Gas Law

Common Mistakes

  • Using Celsius instead of Kelvin.
  • Using the wrong gas law.
  • Forgetting to rearrange formulas correctly.
  • Using inconsistent units.
  • Using the wrong value of R.
  • Confusing STP conditions with ordinary room conditions.

Lesson Summary

  • The Combined Gas Law relates pressure, volume, and temperature when moles remain constant.
  • The Ideal Gas Law combines pressure, volume, temperature, and moles into one equation.
  • Temperature must always be expressed in Kelvin.
  • The gas constant R links the variables in the Ideal Gas Law.
  • STP is defined as 273 K and 1 atm.
  • At STP, one mole of an ideal gas occupies approximately 22.4 L.
  • Gas laws allow scientists and engineers to predict gas behavior in complex systems.
