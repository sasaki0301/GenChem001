The Periodic Table: Groups, Families, and Periods

The periodic table is a map of the elements. Its rows and columns reveal patterns and help us locate elements efficiently.

1. Read the periodic-table map

Elements are arranged by increasing atomic number, which is the number of protons in the nucleus. A group is a vertical column; a period is a horizontal row. Modern groups are numbered 1–18, and periods are numbered 1–7.

A full periodic table has 18 groups. The lanthanides and actinides are often shown as two detached rows to save space; they belong within periods 6 and 7.

Read an element tile

In the map, chlorine is shown as 17 Cl. Cl is its chemical symbol and 17 is its atomic number: every chlorine atom has 17 protons. Many full tables also show the element’s name and relative atomic mass. Atomic number and relative atomic mass are different quantities.

2. Groups and families

A group is a vertical column. Elements in the same group often have related chemical properties; for many main-group elements, this is related to the arrangement of outer electrons. There are exceptions, so use “often” and “generally” rather than assuming identical behavior.

Family Group Examples General description
Alkali metals 1, excluding hydrogen Li, Na, K Reactive metals; often form +1 ions in common compounds.
Alkaline earth metals 2 Be, Mg, Ca Metals; often form +2 ions in common compounds.
Halogens 17 F, Cl, Br Reactive nonmetals; some commonly form −1 ions.
Noble gases 18 He, Ne, Ar Gases that are relatively unreactive under ordinary conditions.
Hydrogen’s special placement: It is usually placed above group 1 but is a nonmetal with distinctive properties. Helium belongs in group 18 because of its chemical behavior, although its outer electron arrangement differs from that of the other noble gases.

Groups 3–12 occupy the central part of the full table and include the transition metals. The lanthanides and actinides are commonly displayed below the main body.

3. Periods: moving across a row

A period runs horizontally. As you move from left to right through a period, atomic number increases. Properties also change across the row; they do not remain identical.

Example · Period 3

Na → Mg → Al → Si → P → S → Cl → Ar

All eight elements lie in period 3. Sodium and magnesium are metals; chlorine and argon are nonmetals. Their different columns mean they belong to different groups.

Worked example · Locate chlorine

  1. Find Cl in the partial table.
  2. Trace upward to the column heading: group 17.
  3. Trace left to the row label: period 3.
  4. Group 17 is the halogen family.
Element properties vary in patterns, but we need to specify which property and direction. In Lesson 3 we will compare atomic radius and atomic mass across periods and down groups.

Show one possible answer

Cl and F share a vertical group and belong to the halogen family, so they have some related chemical properties. Cl and Na share a horizontal period, but they are in different groups and do not have the same family properties.

6. Key terms and takeaways

  • Atomic number: the number of protons; the basis of the table’s order.
  • Group: a vertical column numbered 1–18; members often have related chemical properties.
  • Period: a horizontal row numbered 1–7.
  • Families: recognize alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens, and noble gases by group.
  • Element tile: use the atomic number and chemical symbol to identify the element.
