1. Read chemical formulas

An element symbol identifies an element. A subscript gives the number of atoms of that element in one molecule or formula unit; no subscript means 1. A coefficient multiplies the entire formula without changing the formula of one entity.

Formula Counting Result
CO2 C = 1; O = 2 per molecule 3 atoms per molecule
2H2O 2 molecules × (2 H and 1 O) 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms total
Ca(OH)2 Ca = 1; O = 1 × 2; H = 1 × 2 1 Ca, 2 O, and 2 H per formula unit
Al2(SO4)3 Al = 2; S = 1 × 3; O = 4 × 3 2 Al, 3 S, and 12 O per formula unit
Apply a subscript outside parentheses to every atom inside them. For an ionic compound such as NaCl, a formula unit gives the simplest whole-number ratio of ions; it is not a discrete molecule.

2. Formula mass and molar mass

Formula mass sums atomic masses for one formula and is expressed in u. Molar mass is mass per mole of specified entities and is expressed in g/mol. Their numerical values correspond when the same atomic-mass data are used, but their units and meanings differ.

Use the following rounded atomic molar masses throughout this review unless otherwise specified. Other periodic tables may give slightly different values.

Element H C N O Na Mg S Cl Ca Al
g/mol 1.01 12.01 14.01 16.00 22.99 24.31 32.06 35.45 40.08 26.98

molar mass = Σ (atom count × atomic molar mass)
mass = moles × molar mass   |   moles = mass ÷ molar mass

Worked example · Ca(OH)2

Ca = 1 × 40.08 = 40.08 g/mol; O = 2 × 16.00 = 32.00 g/mol; H = 2 × 1.01 = 2.02 g/mol. Total molar mass = 74.10 g/mol.

For 2.00 mol, mass = 2.00 mol × 74.10 g/mol = 148.2 g ≈ 148 g to three significant figures. The coefficient or amount changes sample mass, not the molar mass of Ca(OH)2.

3. Percent composition by mass

Mass percent compares the mass contributed by one element with the total mass of the compound. Count atoms, calculate each element’s contribution per mole, divide by total molar mass, and multiply by 100%.

% element = (element mass contribution ÷ compound molar mass) × 100%

Worked example · CaCO3

Per mole of CaCO3: Ca contributes 40.08 g; C contributes 12.01 g; and O contributes 3(16.00) = 48.00 g. M = 100.09 g/mol.

Element Calculation Mass percent
Ca 40.08 ÷ 100.09 × 100% 40.04%
C 12.01 ÷ 100.09 × 100% 12.00%
O 48.00 ÷ 100.09 × 100% 47.96%

The percentages total 100.00% after rounding. In a pure 50.0 g sample, the oxygen mass is approximately 0.4796 × 50.0 = 24.0 g to three significant figures.

For a complete pure compound, elemental mass percentages add to about 100%. Compare atom count × atomic molar mass, not atom count alone, to determine which element contributes more mass.

4. Find empirical formulas

An empirical formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. Different molecular formulas can share the same empirical formula; for example, C6H12O6 reduces to CH2O.

  1. Find the mass of each element. For percent data, an assumed 100 g sample converts each percentage numerically into grams.
  2. Convert each mass to moles: grams ÷ atomic molar mass.
  3. Divide every mole amount by the smallest amount.
  4. If needed, multiply all ratios by a common small integer; write the simplest whole-number subscripts.
  5. Check the formula against the data, allowing for measurement and rounding.

Worked example · Iron oxide

A sample of an iron oxide contains 69.94% Fe and 30.06% O. Use Fe = 55.85 and O = 16.00 g/mol and assume 100.00 g.

Fe = 69.94 ÷ 55.85 ≈ 1.252 mol; O = 30.06 ÷ 16.00 ≈ 1.879 mol. Divide by 1.252 to obtain Fe : O ≈ 1.00 : 1.50. Multiply both terms by 2: 2 : 3. The empirical formula is Fe2O3.

A ratio near 1.5 suggests multiplying all values by 2; a ratio near 1.33 may suggest multiplying by 3. Do not round a substantial fraction to the nearest integer independently or force inconsistent measurements into a formula.

5. Find molecular formulas

A molecular formula gives the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule. Its subscripts are a common positive whole-number multiple of the empirical subscripts.

n = molecular molar mass ÷ empirical-formula molar mass
molecular formula = empirical-formula subscripts × n

Worked example · From CH2O

A molecular compound has empirical formula CH2O and measured molar mass 180.18 g/mol. Its empirical-formula molar mass is 12.01 + 2(1.01) + 16.00 = 30.03 g/mol.

n = 180.18 ÷ 30.03 = 6. Multiply every subscript by 6 to get C6H12O6. Check: its molar mass is 180.18 g/mol and its subscripts reduce to 1 : 2 : 1.

The multiplier can be 1. If the calculated quotient is not reasonably close to a whole number, check measurements, formula, arithmetic, and units. Different structural isomers can share a molecular formula, so composition and molar mass alone do not establish a unique structure.

Challenge · Connect composition and formulas

A molecular compound contains only carbon and hydrogen: 85.60% C and 14.40% H by mass. Its molecular molar mass is 56.12 g/mol. Using C = 12.01 and H = 1.01 g/mol, find its empirical and molecular formulas.

Show one solution

For 100.00 g: C ≈ 85.60 ÷ 12.01 = 7.13 mol; H ≈ 14.40 ÷ 1.01 = 14.26 mol. Divide by 7.13 to get approximately 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH2. Its empirical-formula molar mass is 14.03 g/mol. n = 56.12 ÷ 14.03 = 4, so the molecular formula is C4H8. Its calculated molar mass is 56.12 g/mol.
