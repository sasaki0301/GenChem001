Chapter Summary: Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry

Chapter Big Idea

Chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms to form new substances. By balancing chemical equations and understanding reaction patterns, chemists can predict how substances react and calculate exactly how much product can be formed. This chapter introduced the principles of chemical reactions, reaction classification, stoichiometric calculations, limiting reactants, and percent yield. These concepts form the foundation of quantitative chemistry and industrial chemical production.

1. Chemical Reactions and Chemical Equations

A chemical reaction is a process in which reactants are transformed into products through the breaking and formation of chemical bonds.

Term Meaning
Reactants Substances present before the reaction
Products Substances formed after the reaction
Chemical Equation Symbolic representation of a reaction

Example

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Hydrogen and oxygen react to form water.

2. Law of Conservation of Mass

The Law of Conservation of Mass states that matter cannot be created or destroyed during a chemical reaction.

The number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of a balanced equation.

Balancing equations ensures that this law is satisfied.

3. Balancing Chemical Equations

Chemical equations are balanced by changing coefficients, never subscripts.

Example

4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃
Steps for Balancing Equations:
  1. Write the correct formulas.
  2. Count atoms on both sides.
  3. Adjust coefficients.
  4. Check atom counts.
  5. Reduce to the smallest whole-number ratio.

4. Major Types of Chemical Reactions

Reaction Type General Pattern
Synthesis A + B → AB
Decomposition AB → A + B
Single Replacement A + BC → AC + B
Double Replacement AB + CD → AD + CB
Combustion Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Examples

Synthesis

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl

Decomposition

2HgO → 2Hg + O₂

Combustion

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

5. Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry is the study of quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a chemical reaction.

All stoichiometric calculations begin with a balanced chemical equation.

Balanced equations provide mole ratios that relate one substance to another.

Example Equation

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃

Possible mole ratios:

2 mol NH₃ 1 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂ 1 mol N₂

6. Stoichiometric Calculation Pathway

Most stoichiometry problems follow this sequence:

Mass → Moles → Mole Ratio → Moles → Mass

Moles act as the central bridge connecting all stoichiometric calculations.

7. Mole-to-Mole Calculations

Using:

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃

Determine the NH₃ produced from 5.00 mol N₂.

5.00 mol N₂ × 2 mol NH₃ 1 mol N₂ = 10.0 mol NH₃

8. Mass-to-Mass Stoichiometry

Mass-to-mass calculations require converting grams to moles, applying a mole ratio, and converting back to grams.

General Pattern:

Mass → Moles → Mole Ratio → Moles → Mass

9. Limiting Reactant

A limiting reactant is the reactant that is consumed completely first.

The limiting reactant determines the maximum amount of product that can be formed.
Term Description
Limiting Reactant Consumed first
Excess Reactant Remains after the reaction

10. Theoretical Yield

Theoretical yield is the maximum amount of product predicted by stoichiometric calculations.

Theoretical Yield = Maximum possible product

It assumes perfect reaction conditions and no product loss.

11. Actual Yield

Actual yield is the amount of product actually obtained from an experiment or industrial process.

Actual Yield = Product collected in reality

Actual yield is usually lower than theoretical yield.

12. Percent Yield

Percent yield measures reaction efficiency.

Actual Yield Theoretical Yield × 100%

A high percent yield indicates an efficient reaction.

If:

  • Actual Yield = 42.5 g
  • Theoretical Yield = 50.0 g
42.5 50.0 × 100 = 85%

Chapter Mastery Check

1. Why must chemical equations be balanced?

To satisfy the Law of Conservation of Mass by ensuring equal numbers of atoms on both sides.

2. What reaction type follows the pattern AB → A + B?

Decomposition Reaction

3. What information do coefficients provide in a balanced equation?

Mole ratios between reactants and products.

4. What is the limiting reactant?

The reactant consumed completely first.

5. What does percent yield measure?

The efficiency of a chemical reaction.

Chapter Summary

  • Chemical equations describe chemical reactions and must be balanced.
  • Balanced equations obey the Law of Conservation of Mass.
  • Five major reaction types are synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement, and combustion.
  • Stoichiometry uses mole ratios from balanced equations to calculate quantities of substances.
  • Limiting reactants determine the maximum amount of product formed.
  • Theoretical yield predicts the maximum product possible.
  • Actual yield represents the product actually obtained.
  • Percent yield measures reaction efficiency and process performance.
